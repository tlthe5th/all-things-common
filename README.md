# All Things Common (`ἅπαντα κοινά`)
> *"From each, to each, as anyone had need — written 1,800 years before Marx."*

An open-source bridge connecting contemporary critiques of economic exploitation to the ancient communal traditions found in the **Septuagint (LXX)** and the **Greek New Testament**.

---

## 1. The Critique of Extraction
The repository frames modern economic struggles—such as wage stagnation, housing shortages, and high costs of living—as symptoms of systemic hoarding. It positions these issues alongside ancient scriptural frameworks that reject permanent land ownership and wealth concentration.

## 2. Scriptural and Historical Foundations
The project highlights several primary texts and principles from the Greek Scriptures:
* **Land Ownership & the Jubilee — Λευιτικόν (Leviticus) 25:10, 23 (LXX):** Affirms that land belongs ultimately to God and establishes a 50-year reset for debts, debt-slavery, and land distribution (*κληρονομία*).
* **Early Christian Community — Πράξεις (Acts) 4:32, 34–35:** Records the early believers holding all things in common and distributing resources based on individual need.
* **The Mission to the Poor — Λουκᾶς (Luke) 4:18 / Ἠσαΐας (Isaiah) 61:1 (LXX):** Emphasizes the proclamation of release to captives and good news to the *πτωχοί* (poor).
* **Voluntary vs. Coerced Sharing — Πράξεις (Acts) 5:4 & Ἰωάννης (John) 4:24:** Distinguishes state-enforced redistribution from internal transformation driven by *μετάνοια* (repentance) and faith (*πίστις*).
